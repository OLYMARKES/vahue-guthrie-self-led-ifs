A wider operating system

Most of us have learned to meet life through focused attention: analysing, planning, naming and trying to solve one thing at a time. Guthrie calls this OS 1.0. It is useful, but it can become overconcentrated and effortful when we rely on it alone.

OS 2.0 adds awareness. Attention can still focus, but it is held inside a wider field that also includes the body, the room, other people and what matters most. The point is not to discard thinking. It is to transcend and include it: focus remains available without having to carry the whole experience.

The direction of the course

This course is not asking participants to create a rare or permanent state. It is helping them notice a capacity that is already present, bring it from the background into the foreground, and gradually increase both the frequency and the duration of access to Self.

When access becomes shallower, parts language offers a simple doorway. “I am rushing” can become “A part of me is rushing.” The situation has not disappeared, but even a small amount of space may return. From there, the participant can relate to the experience rather than be completely defined by it.

Possible placement: Lesson 1 as the course frame, or Lesson 2 as the bridge from attention to awareness.

Open full candidate material
Candidate seven-minute practice
  1. Arrive. Find an upright, comfortable position. Let the spine support you without becoming rigid. Eyes may close or rest in a soft gaze.
  2. Ground. Feel the feet and the weight of the body meeting the seat. Let gravity carry what it can carry. Notice any sense of stability or stillness.
  3. Core. Bring light attention to the torso: belly, chest, back and throat. Feel the body from within without changing what you find.
  4. Field. Allow awareness to open into the room. Include sound, peripheral vision and the body together. Thoughts and sensations may come and go inside this wider field.
  5. Focus. Let attention rest lightly on one simple thing while ground, core and field remain available. Focus does not need to collapse the wider awareness.
  6. Return. Notice how much access to Self may be present: none you can identify, a little, or more. Complete the sentence, “Access to Self may feel like…”

This could become a Guthrie-recorded audio with transcript and captions. The final language should preserve his pacing and be approved before publication.

Safety boundary: every invitation is optional. If there is strain or disorientation, open the eyes, orient to the room and stop.

What IFS means by Self

IFS proposes that Self is not another part and is not something a person has to manufacture. It is an innate capacity for awareness and relationship. It may be obscured when protective parts are active, but in the IFS model it is not damaged or lost.

Self can be understood as the seat of awareness: the capacity that can notice, hold and understand experience. Parts are contents of consciousness; Self is the awareness able to be in relationship with them.

How access may be recognised

The familiar eight Cs — calm, clarity, curiosity, compassion, confidence, courage, creativity and connectedness — are signals, not a checklist participants must perform. Access may also show up more quietly: a little more space, an easier breath, less urgency, softer focus, or the ability to stay curious without forcing an outcome.

Self-leadership does not mean getting rid of parts. It means that parts can be seen and valued without having to run the whole system. The relationship changes before the experience necessarily changes.

Week 1 could use the first two paragraphs and a short marker list. The fuller 8 Cs and Self-leadership teaching could be developed later.

The Six Organizing Principles of IFS

Source: old Week 1 handout in English and Russian
Open full candidate material
A light systems frame for participants
  1. We have many parts. Different inner voices, impulses and positions are a natural feature of the mind.
  2. Parts are not bad. Even when their behaviour creates difficulty, they usually carry an understandable protective intention.
  3. Parts live in relationship. They form patterns, react to one another and try to keep the system safe or balanced.
  4. We are more than our parts. IFS calls the aware, relational centre of the system Self.
  5. Self is the guiding direction. More access to Self makes curiosity, clarity and compassionate leadership more available.
  6. Trust the pace of the system. Change is invited through relationship and respect rather than force.

For Week 1, these principles can function as an orientation map. They explain why the course uses parts language and why the participant is never asked to battle, suppress or diagnose an inner experience.

This adaptation deliberately removes practitioner instructions, client language and advanced material about exile work or unburdening.

Awareness experiments and release points

Placed by stage

The bowl-of-soup experiment belongs in the Week 1 live session with debrief. Walking and peripheral-vision practice appear only after Guthrie introduces them. The fingers variation is held back.

Source: July 29 call exercise bank
Open full candidate material
1. Peripheral vision · after live, if introduced

Soften the eyes and notice how much of the room can be included without strain. Compare a calm moment with an ordinary busy moment. The earlier fingers variation is not proposed for this course.

2. Three channels while walking · after live

In an ordinary, safe place, walk first with attention on seeing. Then let hearing become more prominent. Then notice sensations in the body. For the final minute, see whether all three channels can be present together without forcing equal attention.

3. The bowl-of-soup test · live session only

Imagine carrying a full bowl across a room, or try the experiment with an unbreakable cup in a safe environment. First notice what happens when attention clamps tightly onto the object. Then find ground, widen the field and allow a light focus on the object. Compare effort, stability and available awareness.

Release rule: do not publish an experiment as independent participant practice until Guthrie has introduced it live. These are observations, not tests to pass.

Possible Week 1 participant package
  • Week guide: the seven short lessons on this page, designed to be read in sequence or revisited individually.
  • Core audio: Guthrie’s seven-minute ground–core–field–focus practice, with transcript and captions.
  • One visual map: attention, awareness, Self and parts on a single page.
  • Live session: a 90-minute call, followed by a replay divided into meaningful chapters rather than one undifferentiated recording.
  • During-the-week experiment: one low-intensity moment each day using “A part of me…” and noticing whether any space appears.
  • Keep with you: a one-page summary of the core distinctions, practice sequence and safety boundaries.
  • Support page: what the course is and is not, when to pause, and where to look for additional support.

Optional production pieces could include a brief welcome video before the week and a short integration note after the live call. The aim is a coherent learning rhythm, not a large volume of files.

This is a menu for production decisions, not a promise that every item must be made.

Keep out of this participant week

Practitioner protocols and peer work

Hold back

Six Fs, triads, guiding another person, memory retrieval, exile work, unburdening and practitioner-competence framing are intentionally excluded from this six-week participant course.

Source: old twelve-week archive · internal reference only
Open full rationale
What exists in the old archive

The previous practitioner course included the Six Fs, practitioner–client–observer triads, guiding another person, memory retrieval, exile witnessing, unburdening and questions of practitioner competence or supervision.

Why it is held back here

Moving those materials directly into this course would change both the audience and the level of responsibility. It could overload a foundational participant week and imply peer therapy or practitioner training that this course does not provide.

What may still be translated safely

Some underlying principles can be expressed in participant language: ask permission, appreciate protective intent, respect stop signals, stay with ordinary low-intensity examples, and do not force access to memories or vulnerable material.

The raw practitioner archive remains an internal reference and is not embedded in this public review page.
